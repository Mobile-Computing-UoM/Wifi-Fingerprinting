# Cross-Device Hardware Transfer & Antenna Offset Calibration Analysis

## 1. Executive Summary & Research Context

According to **Section 6 (Option E)** of the CS4473 Mobile Computing Research Project guidelines and our research proposal ("Our Own Twist"):
> *"Is cross-device localization feasible when a fingerprint database surveyed on one smartphone model is transferred directly to a different smartphone model without re-surveying, and how do hardware-specific RSSI offsets impact accuracy?"*

Building reference radio maps via dense offline site surveys is labor-intensive. If a survey map recorded by a single surveyor device can be shared directly with arbitrary user smartphones, the operational cost of indoor positioning drops to zero. However, physical radio frontends differ across manufacturers:
* **Antenna Sensitivity & Gain**: Variance in PCB antenna trace geometry, ground plane dimensions, and casing attenuation.
* **Chipset & LNA Implementation**: Differences in Low-Noise Amplifier (LNA) gain steps, Automatic Gain Control (AGC), and driver RSSI reporting between different silicon vendors.

This study empirically evaluates the feasibility of **cross-device radio map transfer** between two completely distinct Android smartphones across **8 reference spots** ($S1$–$S8$, 2.0 m grid spacing) under three experimental conditions:
1. **Condition 1 (Phone A Baseline)**: Same-device localization (Samsung Galaxy A20e on Samsung survey map).
2. **Condition 2 (Phone B Uncalibrated)**: Raw cross-device transfer (OPPO A16 on Samsung survey map without offset correction).
3. **Condition 3 (Phone B Calibrated)**: Cross-device transfer with a single-spot antenna sensitivity offset calibration.

## 6. Speculated Reasons & Physical Radio Insights

### 6.1 Why Uncalibrated Cross-Device Transfer Degraded (45% $\to$ 25%)
1. **Asymmetric Antenna Attenuation**:
   - The uncalibrated transfer suffered a **20.0% absolute drop in accuracy** (falling from $45.0\%$ on Phone A down to $25.0\%$ on Phone B).
   - This was not caused by a uniform shift across all APs, but by **frequency and direction-dependent sensitivity differences**: Phone B measured AP `anon_44ac` and AP `anon_bc94` $\approx 5\text{ dB}$ weaker than Phone A, while simultaneously measuring AP `anon_e7b3` $\approx 2\text{ dB}$ stronger.
   - In a Euclidean distance metric $\sum (s_d - r_d)^2$, having two dimensions shifted downward by $5\text{ dB}$ and two dimensions shifted upward by $2\text{ dB}$ warps the vector space by over $(5)^2 + (5)^2 + (2)^2 + (2)^2 \approx 58\text{ dB}^2$. This severe distortion pulls nearest-neighbor matching away from true ground truth.
2. **Chipset LNA & AGC Characteristics (MediaTek vs. Exynos)**:
   - The Samsung Galaxy A20e uses an Exynos 7884 Wi-Fi subsystem, whereas the OPPO A16 uses a MediaTek Helio G35 Wi-Fi frontend.
   - Low-cost MediaTek transceivers often implement more aggressive power-saving and different noise-floor floors (reflected in Phone B detecting intermittent AP `anon_d856` that was below Phone A's reporting threshold).

### 6.2 Why One-Spot Calibration Succeeded (25% $\to$ 39% Accuracy, 2.97 m $\to$ 2.18 m Error)
1. **Re-aligning the Global Signal Scale**:
   - Performing a rapid 10-scan calibration at spot $S1$ computed the central antenna offset and adjusted live test queries:
     $$\mathbf{s}_{\text{corrected}} = \mathbf{s}_{\text{raw}} - \Delta_{\text{offset}}$$
   - This single adjustment restored classification accuracy from **$25.0\%$ back to $39.0\%$** (recovering nearly $75\%$ of the lost performance) and pushed the mean distance error down to **$2.18\text{ m}$** (which actually improved upon Phone A's baseline of $2.66\text{ m}$).
2. **Dramatic Recovery at Problem Spots**:
   - At spot **`S2`**: Accuracy was restored from $60\%$ back to **$100.0\%$ ($0.00\text{ m}$ error)**.
   - At spot **`S7`**: Accuracy surged from $0\%$ to **$80.0\%$**, and mean distance error collapsed from **$6.17\text{ m}$ down to $0.40\text{ m}$**!
   - At spot **`S4`**: Mean distance error dropped from **$4.80\text{ m}$ down to $2.80\text{ m}$**.

### 6.3 Remaining Discrepancies & Limitations
* **Multipath & Human Body Shielding**: At spots `S1` and `S3`, both Phone A and Phone B exhibited misclassifications due to strong AP `Galaxy A01` being obstructed by the user's torso during test sweeps.
* **Non-Linear Antenna Gain**: While a scalar antenna offset $\Delta_{\text{offset}}$ brings substantial improvement, real-world smartphone antennas have non-isotropic 3D radiation patterns. When holding Phone B in a slightly different hand grip, directional gain varies across azimuth angles.
